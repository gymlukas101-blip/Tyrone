# Facial animation audit and Animation 2.0 changes

Released in v0.3.81 (merged from `feature/animation-2`). `?face=baseline` still shows the old face for A/B.

**Scope:** Monkey's face while he listens, thinks and talks. Body clips, gestures and the sleep chain are unchanged.

**Primary artistic reference:** Donkey Kong Bananza.
- I could not watch the reference videos from this container (see `DK-BANANZA-ANIMATION-REFERENCE-ANALYSIS.md`).
- The acting rules below therefore come from general character-animation principles, not frame study.

## 1. How the face worked before (facts from the code)

| Part | Before (`legacyFace` in `ui/src/avatar3d.ts`) |
|---|---|
| **Visemes** | The Python speaker runs one FFT per 23 ms audio block (`monkey/audio/visemes.py`).<br>That gives 15 Oculus viseme weights, guessed from loudness and spectrum only. |
| **Viseme easing** | `now += (target - now) * 0.6` (attack) or `* 0.3` (release), **per rendered frame**. |
| **Jaw** | `min(0.6, level * 0.8)`, eased per frame (0.5 / 0.25).<br>Added on top of whatever the vowel viseme already opens. |
| **Expressions** | The `expr` preset of the current emote clip, eased `* 0.12` per frame.<br>Written after the visemes and independent of them. |
| **Overrides** | Yawn and blink are written last. |
| **Acting from intent** | None. The face only changed when a body emote clip played; talking used one neutral face for every sentence. |

## 2. Defects found

1. **Speed depended on frame rate.**
   - Every ease was a fixed fraction per frame. The render tiers (Milestone 4) draw idle at 30 fps and talking at the display rate (60–144 Hz).
   - So the mouth moved up to about 5× faster on a 144 Hz monitor than at 30 fps. Expression changes snapped or crawled depending on the screen.
2. **Jaw and vowel double-counted.**
   - On "AH", both the `viseme_aa` shape and `jawOpen` opened the mouth.
   - Loud open vowels over-opened, and the mouth looked rubbery.
3. **Moods fought the mouth.**
   - A smile or `mouthO` from an emote was written at full strength over speech.
   - Lip closures (M/B/P) had to compete with a lip-parting mood. A "surprised O" stacked with the `viseme_O` shape.
4. **No acting.**
   - Nothing reacted to *what* he was saying, so "Well, actually…" and "Whoa, nice!" got the same face.
   - Listening and thinking had no resting attitude of their own.
5. **Lip shapes were guesses from sound alone.**
   - A spectrum can hear loudness and hiss, but it can't tell "oh" from "ee" reliably, and it guesses closures.

## 3. What changed

| Change | File | Fixes defect |
|---|---|---|
| **`FaceMixer`**: every facial morph is decided in one place, from layers (speech, seal, acting, emote, override).<br>Easing uses time constants in seconds: viseme 35 / 70 ms, jaw 45 / 90 ms, mood 160 ms (120 ms while talking). | `ui/src/avatar/face.ts` | 1 |
| The jaw is reduced by how much the vowel shape already opens: `× (1 − 0.45·opening)`. | `face.ts` | 2 |
| **Lip seal**: on `viseme_PP` (or silence mid-speech), lip-parting moods (`bigGrin`, `grimace`, `mouthO`, `tongueOut`) fully release.<br>A closed-lip smile curve is kept at 40%, so warmth survives.<br>Mouth moods are scaled down while the mouth is busy talking. `mouthO` can't stack past fully round. | `face.ts` | 3 |
| **`PerformanceDirector`**: picks at most one reaction per sentence from the words he's about to say (skeptical, curious, surprised, excited, sympathetic, warm, proud).<br>Each has phases: anticipation, hit, hold, settle.<br>There's a 2.4 s cooldown, and a new state drops a stale reaction.<br>Resting attitudes: attentive while listening; one brow up and a slight squint while thinking. | `ui/src/avatar/acting.ts` | 4 |
| **Text-assisted lip sync**: the sentence's spelling becomes a syllable sequence (onset consonant + vowel shape). Each new loudness burst in the audio advances one syllable.<br>Lips close in the gap before a p/b/m syllable. The result is blended 65/35 with the spectrum estimate, so audio still drives openness and hiss. | `monkey/audio/lipsync.py`, `monkey/audio/speaker.py` | 5 |
| **Deterministic `Avatar3D.step(dt)`** (virtual clock) for frame-exact renders and tests. | `ui/src/avatar3d.ts` | (tooling) |
| **`?face=baseline`** keeps the old face for A/B comparison and as a rollback. | `avatar3d.ts` | (rollback) |

Unchanged:
- the voice and TTS engines;
- the audio timing (the text is attached to the sentence before playback, so no extra latency);
- body clips, gestures, the sleep chain, VFX and SFX.

## 4. Before / after evidence

All renders use Monkey's real `monkey.glb` in the app's own Three.js renderer: headless Chromium with SwiftShader, so the lighting and fur are the shipped ones. The renders are not committed to this public repo; they're regenerated by the tools below.

- **Face sheet** (`python -m tools.anim.face_sheet OUT.png`): 7 held poses, old vs new, as face close-ups.
  - **M closure mid-smile:** the new face presses the lips flatter. Both versions keep the lips shut with these inputs.
  - **Talking "EE", skeptical:** the new face has the lifted brow and squint; the old face has a neutral talking face.
  - **Listening and thinking:** the new face has a subtle resting attitude.
  - "OO while surprised" and "AH with a smile" look nearly the same in a still. The differences there are in motion and timing.
- **15 s dialogue demo** (`python -m tools.anim.dialogue_demo OUT_DIR`):
  - The scene: listening → thinking → "Well, actually… that essay is due Friday, not Monday." → "Whoa, you already finished it? Nice!" → back to listening.
  - It's side by side, full body plus a face close-up row, at 30 fps with the speech audio.
  - The voice is espeak-ng, a robotic stand-in (no paid TTS, and Monkey's real voice is not replaced).
  - Frames are stepped deterministically, so audio and picture line up by construction.

Honest caveats:
- At normal app size the face is about a quarter of the avatar's height (roughly 150 px). Most changes are subtle unless you look at the face, which is why the demo has a close-up row.
- The tuning was done by eye on these renders, not against Bananza footage.
- Lip-sync accuracy (does the shape match the phoneme?) has **not been measured** against ground-truth phoneme timing. Measuring it needs an aligner (for example Montreal Forced Aligner or a Whisper word-timing model), and those model downloads are blocked from this container.
- Nothing here has run on the owner's Windows PC yet.

## 5. Methods considered

| Method | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Spectrum-only visemes (before) | Kept as the timing/openness signal; too vague for shape alone |
| **Text syllables timed by audio bursts (chosen)** | Free, offline, about 0 ms extra latency, cheap enough for the audio callback, and works with every voice engine (Fish, Pocket TTS, SAPI) |
| TTS-provided phoneme/viseme timing (e.g. Azure visemes, ElevenLabs alignment) | Precise, but Fish (Monkey's established voice) doesn't return alignment. Switching vendors would replace his voice, which is ruled out without approval |
| Forced alignment per sentence (MFA, wav2vec2 CTC) | Accurate, but adds hundreds of ms per sentence on CPU, and the model downloads are blocked here. A good offline *measurement* tool later, not a runtime path |
| Audio-to-face neural models (NVIDIA Audio2Face, etc.) | Heavy GPU dependency and licensing; the model only has 15 visemes plus expression morphs, so the gain is small |
| LLM-chosen expressions per sentence | Adds latency and cost per sentence; regex intent cues are instant and predictable |
